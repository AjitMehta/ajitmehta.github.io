Research

My research focuses on gravitational-wave astronomy, with an emphasis on advanced search pipelines, compact-binary population inference, and tests of General Relativity using data from the LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA (LVK) network and future detectors such as LIGO–India.

Search Pipelines & Higher Harmonics

I am a core developer of the IAS–HM search pipeline, an independent analysis that incorporates higher-order harmonics and mode-by-mode filtering. This improves sensitivity to asymmetric and higher-mass binary black holes and provides an important cross-check of LVK results.

Population Inference of Binary Black Holes

I work on hierarchical Bayesian inference of binary black-hole populations, aiming to constrain the mass, spin, and redshift distributions, the pair-instability mass gap, and the contribution of hierarchical mergers. These studies connect gravitational-wave observations to the lives of massive stars and the cosmic star-formation history.

Tests of General Relativity & Systematics

A key theme of my work is testing General Relativity using gravitational-wave signals while carefully accounting for waveform modelling uncertainties, detector artefacts, and other systematics. I am particularly interested in inspiral-based null tests, lensing signatures, and robust frameworks that can be applied to future, more sensitive observing runs.

Future Detectors & LIGO–India

Looking ahead, I am interested in how current methods must evolve for next-generation detectors and the addition of LIGO–India to the global network. Improved sky localization, distance measurements, and event rates will enable much more precise astrophysical and cosmological inference.
