Research

My research uses gravitational-wave observations of compact-binary mergers—especially binary black holes— to probe astrophysics across cosmic time and to test fundamental physics in the strong-field, dynamical regime. A major theme of my work is building end-to-end, robust analysis pipelines: from waveform modeling and searches in real detector noise to population inference and precision tests of General Relativity.

Search pipelines & discovery

I work on methods that detect compact-binary mergers buried in real, non-ideal detector noise, with an emphasis on higher-order harmonics (higher modes) and computational efficiency.

  • Improving sensitivity for challenging sources (unequal masses, high masses, high redshifts).
  • Methods that reduce cost without compromising robustness.
  • Bridging detection and downstream population/physics inference.

Population inference

I use hierarchical Bayesian inference to learn the mass–spin–redshift distribution of compact binaries and connect the observed catalog to formation channels and cosmic evolution.

  • Including both confident and marginal events in a principled way.
  • Accounting for selection effects and search systematics.
  • Interpreting outliers (e.g., upper-mass-gap candidates, unequal-mass systems).

Tests of General Relativity

Compact-binary mergers provide an exceptional laboratory for testing GR in the strong-field regime. My focus is on tests that remain reliable as catalogs grow and subtle systematics matter.

  • Theory-agnostic inspiral tests with flexible waveform choices.
  • Systematics control: missing physics, noise drifts, and environment.
  • Extending tests to include precession/eccentricity where needed.

Gravitational lensing of GWs

Strongly lensed GWs can reveal multiple “images” of the same merger. This enables unique science: dark-matter halo structure, lens reconstruction, and precision cosmology.

  • Bayesian identification of lensed pairs in catalogs.
  • Synergy with wide-field surveys (e.g., Euclid/LSST) for lens/source follow-up.
  • Toward event localization within the host galaxy for lensed systems.

Research themes

From foundations (waveforms, searches) to inference and precision physics

1) Efficient waveform modeling for discovery and inference

Matched-filter searches require large banks of waveform templates. My work spans both fast phenomenological models for searches and more accurate models for parameter inference, with a continuing effort to close the gap between “what is detectable” and “what is inferable.”

  • Higher modes and other effects can boost astrophysical reach, but must be handled efficiently.
  • Forward direction: eccentric waveforms suitable for searches, not only for parameter estimation.

Why eccentricity?

Some formation channels predict measurable eccentricity even around ~20 Hz, so eccentric searches can reveal binaries that quasi-circular searches may miss.

2) Robust compact-binary population inference

Population analysis maps observed distributions (masses, spins, eccentricity, redshift) to astrophysical models. A key part of my agenda is making this inference robust as catalogs expand and selection/systematics effects become the limiting factor.

  • Re-estimating population properties when new events are added by advanced searches.
  • Quantifying robustness against waveform uncertainties, noise properties, and astrophysical priors.
  • Interpreting the “upper-mass-gap” and other unusual regions of parameter space.

Methodology focus

Hierarchical modeling, careful selection effects, and cross-checks with alternate waveform and noise assumptions.

3) Precision tests of General Relativity with controlled systematics

My goal is to strengthen GR-testing frameworks so that they remain trustworthy in the era of hundreds of detections, where small modeling omissions or detector non-stationarity can masquerade as new physics.

  • Theory-agnostic inspiral tests that can be extended to higher PN orders.
  • Extensions to include eccentricity and precession when needed.
  • Accounting for detector and astrophysical environmental effects (e.g., microlensing).

CMI synergy

Strong connections to post-Newtonian theory and waveform developments enable sharper and more reliable tests.

4) Gravitational-wave lensing: dark matter, cosmology, and host-galaxy localization

Strong lensing can produce multiple GW signals from the same merger. Identifying these systems and combining GW posteriors with deep imaging surveys opens a path to lens reconstruction, dark-matter halo probes, and precision cosmology.

  • Catalog-level Bayesian identification of strongly lensed pairs.
  • Joint use of GW catalogs and lensing-survey catalogs to refine sky position and redshift.
  • Long-term goal: localize lensed events within the host galaxy (near lens caustics) for precision inference.

Survey synergy

Wide-field lensing surveys (e.g., Euclid/LSST) can help identify the lens and source galaxies within the GW localization region for candidate lensed events.
